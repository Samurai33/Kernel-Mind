## 🎯 What is KernelMind?

KernelMind is a **neuroscience-informed instrumental album** specifically designed for software developers. It combines ambient electronic music with **binaural beats** to potentially enhance focus, creativity, and productivity during coding sessions.

### 🔬 The Science
- **Beta waves (12-20 Hz)**: Associated with attention and concentration
- **Alpha waves (8-10 Hz)**: Promote relaxed awareness  
- **Gamma waves (~40 Hz)**: Support executive functions and problem-solving
- **Theta waves (6 Hz)**: Facilitate deep reflection and recovery

> **Note**: While research on binaural beats shows mixed results, recent studies suggest benefits when integrated with well-crafted musical compositions.

### 🎨 The Concept

Each track represents a **system process or mental state** that developers experience:

```
System Boot → Active Development → Problem Solving → Recovery → Restart
```

```javascript

  track: 'overclock',
  binauralFreq: 18, // Hz
```

```javascript
const config = {
```

## � Research

### Scientific Foundation

KernelMind is built on peer-reviewed neuroscience research:

<details>
<summary><strong>📊 Key Studies & Findings</strong></summary>

#### Binaural Beats & Cognition
- **Lane et al. (1998)**: Beta frequencies (16-24 Hz) improved vigilance performance
- **Colzato et al. (2017)**: 40 Hz gamma enhanced creative thinking
- **Perez et al. (2022)**: 15 Hz + preferred music improved performance in athletes

#### Mixed Evidence & Transparency  
- **García-Argibay et al. (2019)**: Meta-analysis found only 5/14 studies supported binaural beat efficacy
- **Individual variation**: ~30-40% of users don't respond to binaural beats
- **Context matters**: Effects stronger when combined with appropriate music

#### Our Approach
- **Conservative claims**: We present potential benefits, not guarantees
- **Transparent methodology**: All research openly documented
- **Individual customization**: Allow users to adjust parameters
- **Fallback design**: Music remains effective without binaural effects

</details>
